Summary of what I worked on for Day 4.

Today I built the testing infrastructure that lets us test the entire transfer engine without needing two actual devices on a real network. Think of it like a flight simulator for our protocol.

Here's what I put together:

- **FakeTransport & FakeConnection:** These are mock versions of our real network layer. Instead of going through actual TCP sockets, two "connections" are just linked by a pair of in-memory queues. When one side sends a frame, it shows up instantly on the other side. This means we can test the full send/receive flow in under a second, with zero network setup.

- **FaultConfig (the cool part):** This is what makes the fakes really useful. You can configure them to simulate bad network conditions:
  - **drop_rate** — a percentage chance that a sent frame just vanishes, like it would on a flaky Wi-Fi connection.
  - **duplicate_rate** — a chance that a frame gets delivered twice, which is a real thing that happens with TCP retransmits at lower layers.
  - **delay_s** — adds a fixed delay before delivery, so we can test timeout behavior.
  - **rng_seed** — makes the randomness deterministic. Same seed = same drops every time, which means our tests are reproducible.

- **FakeDiscovery:** Instead of broadcasting UDP packets to find other devices, peers are injected manually. I also added a helper to inject "stale" peers (ones that haven't been seen in a while), so we can verify that the liveness timeout filtering works correctly.

Wrote 21 tests covering the basic lifecycle, all three fault modes, deterministic behavior, peer injection, stale eviction, and dedup. All 100 tests in the repo pass green.
